The Certificate III in Plumbing (CPC32420) is the trade qualification that makes you a qualified plumber, and the Certificate IV in Plumbing and Services (CPC40920) is the higher qualification that most regulators ask for before you can hold a full licence, supervise others or contract in your own right. Both can be gained through RPL if your experience covers the units. This guide explains the difference, how RPL works for each, and what to check with your state's plumbing regulator.

In short: the Cert III makes you a plumber; the Cert IV is usually what lets you take responsibility for the work.

The Cert III in Plumbing through RPL

RPL for the Certificate III in Plumbing (CPC32420) suits people who have done a broad range of plumbing work under a licensed plumber but never completed the qualification. That includes apprentices who didn't finish, long-term plumber's offsiders who did the work themselves under supervision, and plumbers who trained overseas.

The qualification covers several areas: water services, sanitary plumbing, drainage, roofing and, depending on electives, gas. Your evidence needs to show real work in each area. If you have mostly worked in one area, such as roofing, the Certificate III in Roof Plumbing may be a closer match.

The Cert IV in Plumbing and Services through RPL

RPL for the Cert IV in Plumbing and Services (CPC40920) suits qualified plumbers with several years of experience after their trade, especially those who already plan and size systems, deal with compliance, supervise apprentices or run their own jobs.

The Cert IV is built around streams, such as water supply, sanitary, drainage, gas, roofing, mechanical services and fire. You choose the units that match the licence classes you want. This is the part people most often get wrong, so check your regulator's requirements before choosing electives.

Match your Cert IV electives to the exact licence classes you want, for example water and sanitary, or gasfitting. The wrong electives can leave you with a qualification your regulator won't accept for that class.

Most regulators look for three things: the right qualification, the right units for each licence class, and evidence of practical experience. Some also set an exam or ask for proof of work done after your trade qualification. Licence names and rules change, so always check the regulator's current website.

What evidence plumbing RPL needs

Plumbing assessors want to see the range and quality of your work. Strong evidence includes:

  • photos and short videos of your installations at different stages, such as rough-in, pressure testing and fit-off;
  • job sheets, invoices and compliance certificates you have prepared or contributed to;
  • plans, sizing calculations and material lists, especially for the Cert IV;
  • reports from licensed plumbers who supervised you, describing the tasks you did;
  • any tickets and licences you already hold.

Licensing in Victoria

Victoria is one of the most common searches for plumbing licences. Victoria separates registration from licensing. Broadly, registered plumbers can carry out work in their class, while licensed plumbers can also take responsibility for and certify work, including work done by others. Moving up generally means holding the relevant Cert IV units and passing the Commission's requirements. Check the Building and Plumbing Commission's current rules for your class before you choose electives.

Which one should you go for?

  • No trade qualification yet, but years of supervised plumbing work? Start with the Cert III in Plumbing.
  • Already qualified and want to certify work, supervise or contract? Go for the Cert IV in Plumbing and Services.
  • Mostly roofing and stormwater? Look at the Cert III in Roof Plumbing.
  • Want to move into building supervision or estimating? The Cert IV in Building and Construction may be a better next step than the plumbing Cert IV.

Common questions about the plumbing pathway

Can I skip the Cert III and go straight to the Cert IV?

Usually not. The Cert IV builds on the trade, and regulators generally expect you to hold the trade qualification and have worked in the trade before moving up.

Is gasfitting included?

Only if you choose the gas units. Gasfitting is usually its own licence class with its own requirements, so plan for it specifically if you need it.

Does RPL count the same as an apprenticeship for licensing?

The qualification counts the same. Regulators may still ask for proof of practical experience, so keep records of the work you have done and who supervised you.
