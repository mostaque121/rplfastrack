If you have worked as a mechanic for a few years without a trade certificate, RPL can recognise your skills with a nationally recognised Certificate III or IV. It is a common path for mechanics who learned on the job, moved from overseas or want to open their own workshop. This guide explains which automotive qualifications are available, what evidence works and how licensing varies between states.

Who uses RPL in the automotive trade

  • Mechanics who learned their trade in a family workshop or on the job.
  • Tradespeople who trained overseas and need an Australian qualification.
  • Experienced mechanics who want to move into diagnosis, management or their own business.
  • Workers moving between light vehicle, heavy vehicle, diesel and body repair.

Automotive qualifications available through RPL

  • Certificate III in Light Vehicle Mechanical Technology, the standard mechanic's trade qualification.
  • Certificate III in Heavy Commercial Vehicle Mechanical Technology for trucks and buses.
  • Certificate III in Automotive Diesel Engine Technology.
  • Certificate III in Automotive Body Repair Technology and the Certificate IV.
  • Certificate III and Certificate IV in Automotive Electrical Technology.
  • Certificate IV in Automotive Mechanical Diagnosis for advanced diagnostics.
  • Certificate IV in Automotive Management and the Diploma of Automotive Technology for workshop leaders.

Evidence that works for mechanics

  • Photos and videos of you servicing, diagnosing and repairing vehicles.
  • Job cards or repair orders you have completed.
  • A reference from a qualified mechanic or workshop manager.
  • Payslips, a statement of service or ABN records.
  • Certificates you hold, such as air conditioning, LPG, or hybrid and EV safety.
  • Overseas trade certificates, translated if needed.

Make sure you appear in some photos or videos, or that your tools and hands are clearly visible. Photos of finished work with no one in them are hard to verify as yours.

A practical assessment, in your workshop or by video, is usually part of the process. Read more about collecting RPL evidence.

Licensing differs by state

Most states do not license individual mechanics, but some regulate repairers. For example, NSW Fair Trading issues motor vehicle repairer licences and tradesperson certificates, and Western Australia has its own certification scheme for motor vehicle repairers. A Certificate III is the qualification those schemes look for. Handling vehicle air conditioning refrigerant needs an ARC licence, which is separate from an RPL qualification.

Check your state requirements before you start.

What the assessment looks like

Your assessor matches your experience to each unit, such as servicing, brakes, steering and suspension, engine and driveline repair, electrical systems and diagnostics. You then have a technical conversation about how you do these jobs: what you check first, how you diagnose a fault, which tools and test equipment you use and how you follow safety procedures.

A practical demonstration is common. It can be a recording of you carrying out a task, a live video observation in your workshop, or an in-person visit. If you have gaps, for example you have never worked on hybrid or electric vehicles, the assessor may ask for extra evidence or suggest a short gap training unit.

Choosing the right qualification

The right qualification depends on the work you actually do:

  • General servicing and repairs on cars and light vans suit the light vehicle mechanical qualification.
  • Trucks, buses and heavy plant suit the heavy commercial vehicle or diesel engine qualifications.
  • Panel and paint work suit the body repair qualifications.
  • Auto electricians suit the automotive electrical qualifications.
  • Workshop managers and senior technicians can look at the Certificate IV or the Diploma.

RPL compared with an apprenticeship

If you have the experience, RPL is far shorter than an apprenticeship. It does not replace practical skill: the assessor still needs to see that you can do the work. Our guide to RPL, apprenticeships and TAFE explains how the paths compare.

Getting started

  1. List your jobs, workshops and the types of vehicles and repairs you have worked on.
  2. Collect photos, job cards and a reference from a qualified person.
  3. Book a free eligibility check to find the right qualification and what gaps there might be.
