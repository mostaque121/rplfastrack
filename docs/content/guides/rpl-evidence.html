Evidence for RPL is anything that proves you can do the tasks in a qualification's units of competency: documents, photos and videos of your work, references from people who have seen you work, and records of earlier training. The better your evidence, the faster and smoother your assessment. This guide explains what assessors look for, what to collect for different kinds of work, and how to organise it so nothing gets missed.

What assessors look for: the rules of evidence

Every assessor in Australia checks evidence against four rules. Keep them in mind as you collect:

RuleWhat it meansHow to meet it
ValidThe evidence relates to the unit being assessedMatch each item to the task it shows
SufficientThere is enough of it to be confidentShow the task more than once, in different situations
AuthenticIt is genuinely your own workBe in the photo or video, and get it verified by a supervisor
CurrentIt reflects how the job is done todayInclude recent work, ideally from the last few years

One piece of evidence rarely covers a unit on its own. Assessors look for a mix, where documents, photos and references back each other up.

The evidence everyone needs

Whatever your industry, start with these:

  • A detailed résumé. List every relevant role with dates, employer, location and the main tasks you did. "Carpenter" isn't enough; "framing, formwork, fit-out and stair construction on residential builds" is.
  • Position descriptions for your roles, if your employer has them.
  • Reference letters or third-party reports. These are written by supervisors, managers or clients and describe what they saw you do. Most RTOs supply a template.
  • Certificates, licences and tickets you already hold, such as a White Card, first aid, forklift licence or earlier qualifications.
  • Proof of identity and your Unique Student Identifier (USI).

Evidence for trades and hands-on work

For trades such as carpentry, plumbing and light vehicle mechanical work, photos and videos are your strongest evidence. Collect:

  • photos of jobs from start to finish, showing several stages, not just the finished result;
  • short videos of you doing key tasks while explaining what you are doing;
  • job cards, work orders, invoices and quotes you prepared;
  • plans, drawings, measurements and material lists you worked from or created;
  • safety documents such as SWMS, JSAs, pre-start checklists and toolbox talk records;
  • test results, inspection records and compliance paperwork;
  • logbooks, if you kept one during an apprenticeship or traineeship.

Make sure you are actually in some of your photos, or that your hands and tools are clearly visible. Photos of finished work with nobody in them are hard to verify as yours.

Evidence for care and community work

For qualifications such as the Certificate III in Individual Support or Cert IV in Ageing Support, privacy matters, so evidence focuses on documents and verification rather than photos of clients:

  • de-identified care plans, progress notes and incident reports you wrote;
  • rosters showing the settings and shifts you worked;
  • training records from your employer, such as manual handling, infection control and medication assistance;
  • supervisor reports describing how you support clients day to day;
  • reflections or case studies describing how you handled real situations, with names removed.

Never upload anything that identifies a client, patient or child. Remove names, addresses, dates of birth and faces before you submit.

Evidence for business, management and office roles

For qualifications such as the Certificate IV in Business or the Diploma of Leadership and Management, your work products are the evidence:

  • reports, plans, budgets and proposals you wrote (remove confidential figures if needed);
  • emails or meeting notes that show you leading, delegating or resolving problems;
  • performance reviews you conducted or received;
  • project documents such as schedules, risk registers and status reports;
  • policies or procedures you developed or improved.

What doesn't work well as evidence

  • Payslips, tax returns and employment contracts on their own. They prove you were employed, not what you can do. They can support your work history, but they don't replace evidence of skill.
  • Generic references. A letter saying you are "reliable and hard-working" says nothing about your skills. Ask referees to describe specific tasks.
  • Very old evidence with nothing recent. Add something current so the assessor knows your skills are up to date.
  • Photos without context. Add a short caption: what the job was, when, and what you did.

How to get a strong reference

A good third-party report is often what turns "not yet competent" into "competent". To get one:

  1. Choose someone who saw your work directly, such as a supervisor, foreman, manager or long-term client.
  2. Give them the RTO's template, or a list of the tasks you want them to comment on.
  3. Ask them to include dates, your role, the tasks you did and how often, and their own role and contact details.
  4. Let them know the assessor may call them to confirm, so they expect it.

Using overseas or older experience

Experience from overseas or from many years ago can still count. The assessor will want to see that it matches Australian standards, so pair it with recent evidence where you can, and be ready to explain in your competency conversation how Australian rules and practices differ from what you learned. Translate any documents that aren't in English, ideally through a certified translator.

How to organise your evidence

Well-organised evidence gets assessed faster. Before you upload:

  • Group files by unit, or by the task list your RTO gives you.
  • Name files clearly, for example "2025-03 bathroom renovation - waterproofing - photo 2.jpg".
  • Write a one-line caption for each photo or video.
  • Keep a simple index listing each file and the unit it supports.
  • Keep copies of everything you send.
