If you already work in aged care, disability support, childcare or community services, RPL can turn that experience into a nationally recognised qualification. Many employers and funding schemes now expect a Certificate III or higher, so a qualification can open up better roles, more hours and supervisor positions. This guide explains the main community services qualifications, what evidence to gather and what to know about screening checks.

Why care workers use RPL

Care work is learned on the job. You may have years of experience helping clients with personal care, supporting people with disability, or looking after children, but no paper qualification. RPL recognises that experience so you do not have to study content you already know.

What evidence to collect

  • A reference or statement of service from your employer or a registered nurse describing your duties.
  • Your position description and payslips showing time in a care role.
  • Training certificates such as manual handling, infection control, medication assistance, first aid and CPR.
  • Examples of progress notes or shift reports, with all client details removed.
  • Your NDIS Worker Screening Check, police check or Working with Children Check, if you hold them.

Privacy matters in care work. Never submit anything that identifies a client. Remove names, addresses and other details, or ask your employer for de-identified examples.

An assessor will usually also observe you at work or speak to your supervisor to confirm your skills. See our evidence guide for more.

Licensing and screening

Care work is not licensed, but employers and funding schemes set their own rules:

  • Many aged care providers now expect at least a Certificate III.
  • NDIS workers in risk-assessed roles need an NDIS Worker Screening Check.
  • A police check is standard across the sector, and roles working with children need a Working with Children Check.
  • Early childhood roles have qualification requirements set by the national regulator, so confirm that the qualification you want is approved for the role.

What about practical placement?

Some community services qualifications normally include work placement hours. When you are assessed through RPL, your existing work in the industry can cover this, but the training organisation decides what it accepts. We confirm this with you at the eligibility check, before you enrol.

How the assessment works for care work

After the eligibility check, you match your experience to the units of the qualification. For a Certificate III in Individual Support that typically means things like supporting independence and wellbeing, working with diverse people, following safe work practices, and communicating and recording client information. Your assessor reviews your evidence and then talks with you, and sometimes your supervisor, about how you do the work day to day.

You may also be asked to complete short scenario questions, for example how you would respond to a fall, a change in a client's condition, or a client who refuses care. If a unit is not fully covered, extra evidence or a short task closes the gap.

Moving up the career ladder

Many workers start with the Certificate III and then step up. A Certificate IV opens team leader and coordinator roles, and a Diploma suits people who plan programs, supervise staff or manage services. Because each step builds on the one before, it is worth deciding where you want to be in a few years and choosing the highest level your experience honestly supports.

Where to start

If you have worked in care for a year or more, start with the Certificate III and see whether you can go further. A free eligibility check will tell you the highest qualification your experience supports. Read how to get RPL in Australia for the full process.
