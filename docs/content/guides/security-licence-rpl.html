A security licence is issued by your state or territory regulator, not by a training organisation, and some regulators limit whether RPL can be used. That makes this one of the areas where checking first matters most. This guide explains how security qualifications and licences fit together, what RPL can and cannot do, and what to confirm in your state before you start.

Qualification versus licence

These are two separate things, and people often mix them up:

  • A qualification, such as the Certificate II in Security Operations, is issued by a registered training organisation (RTO) after you are assessed as competent.
  • A licence is issued by a state or territory regulator (often the police or a fair trading body) once you meet their requirements. These usually include an approved qualification, a criminal history check, first aid and other conditions.

Holding a qualification does not give you a licence. The regulator decides.

Can you use RPL for security training?

It depends on the state and the licence class. Regulators set strict rules about approved training, and some restrict whether RPL can be used for the training needed for a licence. Some accept RPL for people who hold a current or recent licence, while others require specific units to be delivered and assessed in a particular way.

Before you start, confirm in writing with your state regulator, or with us, whether RPL is accepted for the licence you want. This avoids paying for an assessment that cannot be used for your licence.

Evidence security officers can use

If RPL is allowed for your situation, assessors look for proof of the actual work. Typical evidence includes:

  • any current or previous security licence, from any state;
  • a statement of service from a security employer;
  • incident reports, patrol logs and shift handovers with details removed;
  • a current first aid certificate;
  • for supervisors and managers, rosters, post orders, risk assessments and training records.

What to check with your regulator

  1. Which licence class you need for the work you want to do.
  2. Which qualification or units the regulator accepts, and from which RTOs.
  3. Whether RPL is accepted for the training component.
  4. Other requirements: identity, residency or work rights, criminal history, first aid and fitness-to-hold-a-licence checks.

How the RPL assessment works for security work

When RPL is allowed, the process follows the same steps as any other qualification. We start with a short eligibility call about your roles, sites and licence history. You then gather evidence and the assessor checks it against each unit, usually followed by a conversation about how you handle real situations such as access control, conflict, emergencies, patrols and reporting.

Assessors are looking for practical judgement as much as paperwork. They may ask what you would do if an intruder alarm activates, how you de-escalate a confrontation, or how you write an incident report. If a unit is not fully covered by your experience, you may be asked for extra evidence or a short task to fill the gap.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Assuming the same rules apply in every state. Licensing is state-based and the rules differ.
  • Paying for an assessment before checking that your regulator accepts it for your licence class.
  • Uploading incident reports with names, addresses or site details left in. Always remove anything that identifies a person or client.
  • Letting a licence lapse and assuming it can be restored through RPL. Regulators decide what happens when a licence has expired.
