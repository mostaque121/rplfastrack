Yes, work experience you gained overseas can count towards RPL in Australia, as long as it is relevant to the qualification and you can prove it. Assessors care about what you can do now, not where you learned it. This guide explains how overseas experience is assessed, what evidence to gather, how translations and references work, and what RPL cannot do for you, such as replace a visa skills assessment.

Does overseas experience count for RPL?

RPL assesses your skills and knowledge against the units of an Australian qualification. It does not matter whether you built them on a site in Manila, in a workshop in Punjab, in a clinic in Nairobi or in an office in London. What matters is that the tasks you did match what the units require, and that your evidence is genuine, current and enough to show you can do the work to Australian standards.

Overseas experience is often strong, especially in trades, hospitality, care and business roles. The extra work is usually in the paperwork: documents may need translating, and some of your references may be hard to reach.

What evidence works for overseas experience

The same rules of evidence apply as for local work: it must be valid, sufficient, authentic and current. Overseas, this typically means:

  • Employment letters or contracts showing your job title, dates and duties.
  • Reference letters from former supervisors or clients. Email or video verification is normally acceptable if a signed letter is hard to get.
  • Trade certificates, diplomas and licences from your home country, with a translation if they are not in English.
  • Photos and videos of your work. These are often the strongest evidence for hands-on trades, and they are not affected by language.
  • Payslips, tax records or business registration that show how long you worked in the role.
  • Work samples such as plans, quotes, reports, recipes, rosters or job cards.

Translations and language

Assessors can only judge what they can read. If a document is not in English, you will usually need an English translation. Many training organisations accept a translation from a professional translator, and some require a certified translation, so ask which they need before you pay for one. Your photos, videos and work samples often speak for themselves.

The competency conversation with your assessor is held in English. You do not need perfect English, but you should be able to explain how you do your work. Some qualifications, such as those in care or training, have language, literacy and numeracy requirements built into the units.

Overseas experience in licensed trades

Licensed trades such as plumbing and electrical work are regulated state by state. An RPL qualification can be an important step, but a licence is issued by the state regulator, which sets its own rules about required qualifications, supervised experience, exams and local regulations knowledge. Check with your state regulator before you assume that a qualification alone is enough. Our guides to the electrical licence and plumbing licence cover the common pathways.

RPL is not a visa skills assessment

RPL with a registered training organisation is not the same as a skills assessment for migration. If you need a skills assessment for a visa, the assessing authority for your occupation sets the requirements. For visa questions, speak to a registered migration agent.

An Australian qualification gained through RPL can be useful for work, study and licensing in Australia. Whether it helps with a visa or a skills assessment depends on the occupation and the assessing authority, so check their requirements first.

Qualifications popular with people who gained experience overseas

  • Certificate III in Carpentry and other construction trades, where photos of past work carry a lot of weight.
  • Certificate III in Commercial Cookery and Certificate IV in Kitchen Management for chefs with international kitchen experience.
  • Certificate III in Individual Support for people who have worked in aged care, disability or home care.
  • Certificate III in Light Vehicle Mechanical Technology for experienced mechanics.
  • Diploma of Leadership and Management for supervisors and managers.
