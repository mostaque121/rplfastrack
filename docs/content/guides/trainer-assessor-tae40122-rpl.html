You can get the TAE40122 Certificate IV in Training and Assessment through RPL if you already train or assess people at work and can show it. This is the qualification trainers and assessors need to work in a registered training organisation (RTO), and it is also valued for workplace trainers and supervisors. This guide explains who qualifies, what evidence an assessor wants, and what to expect during an RPL assessment.

What is the TAE40122?

The Certificate IV in Training and Assessment (TAE40122) is the current entry qualification for vocational trainers and assessors in Australia. It replaced the older TAE40116. It covers designing and planning training, delivering sessions, assessing learners and working with industry.

It is the qualification many employers, RTOs and TAFE institutes look for. If you plan to teach a vocational qualification in a trade or other field, you also need to hold the qualification you teach, or be able to show the relevant vocational competence and current industry skills.

Who can use RPL for the TAE40122?

RPL suits people who already do the work of a trainer or assessor, even if they have never held the title. For example:

  • workplace trainers who induct, coach and sign off new staff;
  • supervisors and team leaders who run toolbox talks, train apprentices or assess competency on the job;
  • people who have taught in schools, colleges, community groups or overseas and now want to move into vocational education;
  • current RTO trainers who started under an older qualification and need the current one.

If you have never trained or assessed anyone, RPL is unlikely to be the right path, because there is nothing to assess. In that case you may need to complete training with practical delivery first.

What evidence an assessor looks for

TAE evidence should show you designing, delivering and assessing training. Good evidence includes:

  • session plans, lesson plans or training programs you have written;
  • recordings of you delivering training, face to face or online;
  • assessment tools you have developed or used, with completed assessment records;
  • learner feedback, attendance records and training evaluations;
  • records of validation or moderation, if you have taken part;
  • your industry qualifications and a CV showing your training duties;
  • a reference from a manager or RTO describing your training role.

The TAE qualification includes practical delivery and assessment. Expect to be asked to deliver or assess a live or recorded session as part of RPL, so keep recordings of real sessions if you can.

How the assessment works

  1. Eligibility check. We talk through your training and assessing experience and confirm that RPL is realistic.
  2. Evidence portfolio. You upload documents, recordings and references, matched to the units.
  3. Competency conversation. The assessor asks about how you plan sessions, adapt to learners and make assessment decisions.
  4. Gap training or extra evidence. If a unit is not fully covered, you may be asked to deliver an extra session or complete a short task.
  5. Outcome. You receive the qualification once all units are competent.

Most of it can be done online.

What the units cover

The assessor will map your evidence to the main areas of the qualification, including:

  • Planning and designing. Working out what learners need, designing a session and choosing resources.
  • Delivering. Running sessions that suit different learners, face to face or online, and checking that learning is happening.
  • Assessing. Choosing methods, gathering evidence, applying the rules of evidence and making and recording decisions fairly.
  • Working with the industry and the system. Meeting the requirements for RTOs, supporting learners with language, literacy and numeracy needs, and keeping your industry skills up to date.

You do not need to have done every part in a formal training job. A supervisor who runs inductions, coaches apprentices and signs off tasks is already doing much of this.

What to do after the Certificate IV

Many trainers go on to the Diploma of Vocational Education and Training to move into design, coordination or leadership roles. If you want to teach a trade, check that you also hold the trade qualification you plan to teach, or that you are eligible for it through RPL.

Common mistakes

  • Submitting only a CV and no sample of your work.
  • Describing yourself as a trainer without recordings or plans that show it.
  • Choosing the qualification before checking whether you hold the vocational competence in the area you want to teach.

If you are not sure whether your experience is enough, a free eligibility check will tell you before you commit.
