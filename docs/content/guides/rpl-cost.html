The cost of RPL depends mainly on the qualification, how many units it has, how the assessment is done and whether you need gap training. There is no single national price. Each registered training organisation (RTO) sets its own fees, so the same qualification can be priced differently from one provider to the next. This guide explains what drives the price, what should be included, and how to compare quotes properly.

We don't publish prices because they depend on your experience and on the RTO that assesses you. A free eligibility check gives you a quote for your situation.

What you are paying for

RPL fees pay for a qualified assessor's time and the RTO's obligations, not for classes. That usually covers:

  • Enrolment and your initial skills review
  • Reviewing your evidence against every unit in the qualification
  • The competency conversation and any practical assessment
  • Quality checks the RTO must complete before issuing a qualification
  • Issuing your testamur and record of results

Because the work is in the assessment, a bigger qualification with more units, or a hands-on trade that needs a practical demonstration, generally costs more to assess than a smaller one.

Is RPL cheaper than studying?

For experienced workers, RPL usually costs less overall than studying the same qualification, mostly because of what you don't lose. You don't take months off work, you don't travel to classes, and you aren't paying to learn things you already do. The qualification at the end is identical.

Studying may be the better choice if you are new to the industry. Without experience, RPL would leave large gaps, and you would end up paying for both an assessment and training. Our comparison of RPL, apprenticeships and TAFE helps you work out which path fits.

Popular qualifications people ask about

Some of the most-asked-about RPL costs are for qualifications where the price varies most between providers:

  • Cert IV in Plumbing and Services (CPC40920): the price depends heavily on which plumbing streams you need for your licence.
  • Cert III Electrotechnology Electrician (UEE30820): assessment includes practical and capstone requirements.
  • Cert IV in Training and Assessment (TAE40122): depends on how much training and assessment you have already done.
  • Cert IV in Building and Construction (CPC40120): often linked to builder licensing, so electives matter.

Can you get government funding for RPL?

Sometimes. Several states fund RPL as part of their subsidised training programs, but each program has its own rules about who qualifies, which qualifications are covered and which providers take part. Eligibility often depends on your residency, your age, the qualifications you already hold and where you live.

Your employer may also pay. Many employers cover RPL because a qualified workforce helps them win contracts and meet industry requirements. If the qualification relates to your current job, it is worth asking.

Work-related self-education costs may be tax-deductible in some situations. That depends on your circumstances, so check with the ATO or your tax agent.

Watch out for "cheap RPL" offers

A very low price can be a warning sign. A genuine RPL assessment takes an assessor real time, because they have to check your evidence against every unit. Be careful if an offer:

  • promises a qualification without any evidence or assessment;
  • guarantees a pass before anyone has looked at your experience;
  • says you can get a qualification you have no experience in;
  • won't tell you which RTO will assess you and issue the qualification.

Qualifications issued without a proper assessment can be cancelled by the national regulator. A cancelled qualification can cost you your job or licence, so the cheapest option can become the most expensive one.

How to compare RPL quotes

When you compare quotes, compare what is included, not just the number:

  1. Is it the current qualification code, with the electives you actually need?
  2. Does the price include the assessment, the competency conversation and any practical component?
  3. What happens, and what does it cost, if there are gaps?
  4. Are there separate fees for enrolment, re-assessment or certificate reissue?
  5. Is a payment plan available?
  6. Who is the RTO, and can you check its registration on the national register?

A clear quote answers all of these. If a provider can't, keep looking.

Other costs to plan for

The qualification may not be the only cost. Depending on your goal, you may also need to budget for:

  • licence application fees charged by your state or territory regulator;
  • short courses or tickets that some licences require, such as a first aid or CPR certificate;
  • time off work for any practical or in-person assessment.
