Yes, you can do RPL online in Australia. For most qualifications, the whole process happens remotely: you upload evidence through a portal, have your competency conversation over a video call, and receive your qualification by post or email. Some hands-on and licensed trades also need a practical assessment in person or in your workplace. This guide explains what an online RPL looks like, which parts can't always be done online, and how to make sure an online assessment is genuine.

How online RPL works

Online RPL follows the same steps as any RPL assessment, just without the travel:

  1. Eligibility check by phone or video. You talk through your experience and the qualification you want.
  2. Enrolment online. You complete forms, confirm your identity and add your Unique Student Identifier (USI).
  3. Evidence upload. You upload documents, photos and videos to the training organisation's portal, organised by unit.
  4. Competency conversation by video call. An assessor interviews you about how you do your job.
  5. Practical evidence. Depending on the qualification, you may record yourself doing tasks, join a live video observation, or have your supervisor complete a third-party report.
  6. Outcome. Your qualification is issued and recorded against your USI.

Because nothing depends on a timetable, you can fit it around shifts, family and travel. Most of the work is gathering evidence, which you do in your own time.

Is an online RPL qualification legitimate?

Yes. A qualification assessed online is the same nationally recognised qualification as one assessed in person, with the same code and title. The certificate does not say "online". What makes it legitimate is that a registered training organisation (RTO) assessed you properly against every unit, not where the assessment took place.

You can check any RTO and any qualification on the national training register at training.gov.au. Look for the RTO's registration and make sure the qualification is on its scope.

Which qualifications can be done fully online?

Qualifications where skills can be shown through documents, recordings and conversation are usually fully online. That covers most business, management, community services, training and many trade qualifications where workplace evidence is strong. Popular examples:

  • Diploma of Leadership and Management online
  • Certificate IV in Business online
  • Diploma of Project Management online
  • Diploma of Community Services online
  • Cert IV in Training and Assessment online, where you record yourself delivering training and assessing
  • Cert IV in Building and Construction online, which is heavy on planning, estimating and supervision evidence

When part of RPL has to be in person

Some qualifications need an assessor to see you work in real time. That is common when:

  • the qualification leads to a licence, such as electrical work, where a capstone or practical assessment is required (see electrical licence through RPL);
  • safety-critical skills must be shown live, for example CPR and first aid practicals;
  • your evidence doesn't show enough practical work, so the assessor asks for a demonstration to fill the gap.

Even then, the in-person part is usually short, and everything else stays online. Some assessors can observe practical tasks by live video if the setup is good enough.

What you need for online RPL

  • A smartphone or computer with a camera and microphone
  • A reliable internet connection for video calls and uploads
  • A way to scan documents. A phone scanning app is fine.
  • Somewhere quiet for your competency conversation
  • Access to your workplace, or past work, to take photos and videos

Tips for recording video evidence

Video is some of the strongest evidence in an online RPL, because it shows you doing the work. To make it count:

  • Start by saying your name, the date and what you are about to do.
  • Keep your face or hands in view, so the assessor can see it is you.
  • Talk through the task as you go: what you are checking, and why.
  • Show safety steps such as PPE, isolation and site setup. Assessors look for them.
  • Keep clips short, one task each, and name each file by the task it shows.
  • Hide client names, addresses and private information.

Online RPL from anywhere in Australia

Because the process is remote, where you live doesn't limit which qualifications you can go for. People in regional and remote areas, FIFO workers and people on rotating rosters often find online RPL the only practical option. Your qualification is national, so it is recognised in every state and territory, although licences are still issued by each state's regulator.

How to spot an online RPL that isn't genuine

Online is convenient, but it also attracts operators who skip the assessment. Be cautious if:

  • no one asks for evidence of your skills, or a video call is never mentioned;
  • you are promised a qualification within days regardless of your experience;
  • you can't find out which RTO will issue the qualification;
  • the qualification is not on that RTO's scope on training.gov.au.

If there was no real assessment, the qualification can be cancelled later, even years after it was issued.

Is online RPL right for you?

Online RPL suits you if you have solid experience, can collect evidence of it, and want to avoid classes and travel. It is less suitable if you are new to the field or prefer face-to-face support, in which case TAFE or an apprenticeship may suit you better.
